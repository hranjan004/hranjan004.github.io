The problem

A transfer function predicts behavior, but a bench circuit adds loading, component tolerance and measurement choices. The task was to connect observed voltage and phase to the model.

How I approached it

I worked through KCL/KVL, superposition and Thévenin/Norton equivalents, then analyzed capacitor and inductor transients using the single-time-constant model.

For sinusoidal filters, I used the supplied measurement data to build gain and phase tables and Bode plots, relating time delay to phase and comparing low-pass and high-pass behavior.

What changed

Produced lab analyses and reports spanning DC equivalents, transient behavior and frequency response.

The work connected amplitude and timing observations to the equations used to design filters.

What I took from it

The connection between equations and measurement became much clearer when I compared transient and frequency behavior. Amplitude and timing observations provide different views of the same circuit.

Engineering decisions

Magnitude is expressed as 20 log10(Vout/Vin). Phase follows from the time shift at the measurement frequency; both the sign convention and the distinction between peak and peak-to-peak values matter.
