The problem

Producers and consumers need to exchange work without racing, while shared resources need a clear rule for admitting readers and writers.

How I approached it

I implemented a circular queue with head, tail and occupancy state guarded by a mutex. Separate condition variables represent available items and available capacity.

I implemented reader-preference, writer-preference and bounded-reader lock modes, tracking active and waiting participants to make admission policy explicit.

What changed

Built reusable queue and lock modules for the concurrent-systems coursework.

Made the tradeoff between throughput and waiting behavior a configurable part of the lock design.

What I took from it

A lock’s waiting policy is part of its behavior. Making that policy explicit helped me reason about contention and the different needs of readers and writers.

Engineering decisions

Waiters recheck their predicates while holding the mutex after waking. A signal announces that state may have changed; it does not reserve an item or grant a lock.

An N-way policy limits reader admission when a writer is waiting. Correctness depends on maintaining the counters and wake-up conditions together.
