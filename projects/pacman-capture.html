The problem

Two agents must collect food, protect home territory and avoid interfering with each other. A fixed offensive or defensive role cannot respond to every board state.

How I approached it

I developed weighted features for food, capsules, visible opponents, teammate distance and return behavior. The policy switches between attack and defense as threats change.

I investigated stop/reverse penalties, narrow corridors and corner traps, and adjusted distance-based behavior when excessive avoidance caused agents to oscillate.

What changed

Implemented a team-agent policy and wrote a technical retrospective explaining its representation, decision rules and limitations.

The result is a concrete study of coordination under competing objectives.

The archive supports implementation and report work, but does not establish a tournament placement or a controlled improvement over a baseline.

Engineering decisions

Maze distance captures walls that Manhattan distance ignores. Feature weights still need care: rewarding distance from a teammate or opponent too strongly can overpower the goal of making progress.
