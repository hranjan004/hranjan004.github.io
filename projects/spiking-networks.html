The problem

In Professor Eshraghian’s Neuromorphic Computing Group at UCSC, I explore how spiking networks preserve useful activity across time while supporting gradient-based learning. Several experiments stalled even when their architectures appeared plausible.

How I approached it

On MNIST and CIFAR-10, I compared rate, Poisson and latency encodings, investigated threshold-dependent batch normalization and tuned membrane decay, firing thresholds and timesteps.

Layer activity, membrane behavior and loss traces helped separate dead neurons from optimizer and normalization failures.

A separate UCF101 prototype combined frozen ResNet18 features with a small spiking classifier. It remains a demonstration with a split-validation concern.

What changed

Built training pipelines and documented failure modes across multiple network configurations.

Moving experiments to Colab Pro reduced reported epoch time from about seven minutes to under two.

No validated accuracy or energy-efficiency advantage over a conventional CNN is claimed.

Engineering decisions

Spike encoding maps image intensity into temporal events. LIF neurons integrate current with membrane decay and emit spikes at a threshold; surrogate gradients approximate the non-differentiable threshold during training.

Spike-health traces, loss behavior and optimizer updates must be checked together. Active neurons alone did not guarantee learning, while normalization changes could leave initialized currents too weak to cross threshold.
