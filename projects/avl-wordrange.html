The opportunity

A word index needs to accept insertions while answering how many entries lie between two keys. An unbalanced tree or a full traversal makes those queries unnecessarily expensive.

What I engineered

I implemented an AVL tree that stores height and subtree size, with rotations maintaining both balance and rank-query metadata.

I checked insertion sequences, rotation order and lexicographic comparisons against expected range counts.

The results

Built the indexed-query coursework around two rank traversals per range count, with explicit checks of subtree-size invariants.

Why it matters

Subtree sizes turn a balanced tree into a rank index. Maintaining them through rotations is what makes range queries efficient and reliable.

Engineering decisions & measurement context

For inclusive bounds, count(keys ≤ upper) − count(keys < lower) handles the boundary correctly. Heights and sizes must be recomputed after rotations in child-before-parent order.
