The problem

Greedy movement can trap an agent near food or a ghost. Planning requires a consistent model of turns, terminal states and the consequences of an action.

How I approached it

I worked on reflex evaluation and adversarial search agents, using food distance, ghost proximity and scared timers to score successor states.

I debugged state-versus-position mistakes, repeated movement and depth handling, then ran repeated games without graphics to inspect outcomes across layouts.

What changed

Produced runnable agents and recorded wins, losses and scores during coursework experiments.

The experiments exposed how strongly performance depends on the layout, evaluator and depth setting.

The recorded runs use different configurations. I do not present a single selected win rate as a general benchmark.

Engineering decisions

Minimax assumes an adversarial opponent; expectimax models chance. Alpha–beta pruning reduces unnecessary search while preserving the minimax decision for the same tree and evaluator.
