The opportunity

Individual electrical panels can produce related anomaly signals. The aim was to turn those signals into one reviewable incident without changing the existing appliance-inference service.

What I engineered

I built three logical panel agents around one Gemma adapter, conditioned on panel identity, and coordinated their outputs into a shared incident.

I fine-tuned with QLoRA, Hugging Face PEFT and TRL on Lambda GPUs, served with vLLM, and connected Respan traces to a Google Sheets review workflow through Nango.

I generated rule-derived training labels by replaying the existing anomaly engine over archived readings.

The results

Delivered a hackathon prototype combining shared-adapter serving, execution traces and a human review path.

Integrated anomaly assistance alongside the existing BiLSTM / ONNX inference service.

Why it matters

One shared adapter keeps the model layer compact; panel identity preserves context. The incident coordinator turns separate signals into one reviewable output.

Engineering decisions & measurement context

Panel identity conditions a shared adapter rather than requiring three separately fine-tuned models. The incident coordinator combines those panel-level outputs into one reviewable event.

QLoRA reduces the fine-tuning memory requirement; vLLM handles serving. Respan traces and the Sheets review workflow expose model behavior, but do not by themselves establish anomaly accuracy.

Training labels came from rule-engine replay because the source anomaly table contained only two verification rows. The prototype does not establish independently measured anomaly accuracy.
