The problem

A useful action may require exploring a risky route before its reward is known. The challenge is to learn from experience while keeping training behavior separate from evaluation.

How I approached it

I implemented action-value updates and epsilon-greedy selection, working through unseen state-action values, terminal states and ties between legal actions.

I used Gridworld and Pac-Man exercises to examine how learning rate, discounting and exploration affect the policy, including the BridgeGrid scenario and feature-based value approximation.

What changed

Built and debugged the learning-agent coursework and compared sampled learning with model-based value iteration.

The work made exploration settings and training versus test episodes explicit experimental choices.

What I took from it

Separating training from evaluation made the experiments easier to interpret. Exploration helps an agent discover useful behavior, but it also changes what a score means while the agent is still learning.

Engineering decisions

The Q update moves the current estimate toward reward plus the discounted best next-state value. A terminal transition has no future action value; handling that case prevents invalid action lookups.
