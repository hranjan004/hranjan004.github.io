The problem

Microgrid Manager needs appliance-level measurements to compare with panel-level estimates. This project tests whether an ESP32-C3 can acquire and process the necessary signals before building the sensing front end.

How I approached it

Benchmarked two-channel continuous ADC acquisition, blocking reads, LEDC loopback timing and fixed-point versus software-float arithmetic in ESP-IDF C.

Designed an isolated 9 V AC transformer front end with a biased divider for voltage sensing. A power-meter proposal adds an SCT-013-030 current clamp; neither sensor has been purchased or wired.

Planned one JSON update per second over Wi-Fi to a Raspberry Pi, forwarding into Kafka with the eGauge field layout. MQTT publication to Home Assistant is a proposed extension, not an implemented integration.

What changed

At 7.68 kS/s configured aggregate sampling, measured 3,846 samples/s per channel with no dropped or foreign samples during a two-second run.

Across 1,289 periods of a 60 Hz LEDC loopback signal, measured a mean period of 16,666.40 µs and a standard deviation of 0.49 µs. Timing measurements were taken with Wi-Fi off.

Fixed-point multiply-accumulate took 38.4 cycles/sample versus 673.1 for software float, a 17.5× speed advantage. Blocking ADC reads took 48.6 µs.

No appliance voltage, power accuracy or end-to-end telemetry latency has been measured. Only proposals and benchmark firmware are complete.

What I took from it

The benchmarks support further firmware development, but they do not establish measurement accuracy. The next steps are the isolated front end, sampling under Wi-Fi load, reference-meter calibration and Pi telemetry.

Engineering decisions

The board is an ESP32-C3-DevKit-RUST with RUST-2 I2C wiring; firmware is flashed from the Pi over USB. C is the current implementation language; a Rust sampling-loop port is a stretch goal.

Targets, not results: real power within 3% of a reference meter above 100 W; frequency agreement within 1 mHz; voltage sag/swell detection within one cycle. Proposed validation loads are a kettle and a fan or laptop charger, using a Kill A Watt P4400 reference.

Home Assistant was absent from the original proposals. Adding MQTT to the custom ESP-IDF firmware is the proposed integration path. No Pi deployment or newer hardware work has been verified.
