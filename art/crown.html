The Crown that Carries Memory

A Technoblade-inspired crown built from cardboard, paper, wire and reused ramen packaging. Its deliberately rectangular shape carries a digital memory into a physical object; the packaging provides both reinforcement and a visible record of resourcefulness.

Artist statement

My work begins with nostalgia and the warmth of childhood imagination. Through cardboard construction and paper lamination, I developed a wearable sculpture that is playful and deeply personal. Inspired by Technoblade's iconic Minecraft crown, the rectangular form carries the memory of watching someone kind and inspirational during my younger years. This piece explores celebration and remembrance, interacting with space by sitting on the body and transforming the wearer into something from a world that genuinely mattered to them. I became interested in how building a relic from ramen cardboard and ballot box paper can bring a feeling back to life. The relationship between play and sincerity is what makes this crown feel real.

Reflection

There is something deeply wonderful about building something with your own hands that connects you to a person who shaped how you saw the world. This project began not with a design concept or an artistic theory but with a feeling. It began with Technoblade, the Minecraft YouTuber whose kindness, humor, and fighting spirit inspired an entire generation of young viewers, myself included. Watching him as a kid felt like being part of something bigger. His iconic crown became a symbol of resilience and joy, and recreating it as a wearable sculpture felt like the most natural and heartfelt direction I could take.

The crown was constructed from a smaller cardboard box that served as the base structure. Because this is a Minecraft inspired piece, the shape is deliberately rectangular, honoring the blocky aesthetic that made that world so recognizable and beloved. The straight edges and flat planes are not a limitation but a defining feature. They carry meaning. They point back to a digital universe that felt like home for so many of us growing up.

For decoration I used ballot box paper, which gave the surface a smooth and almost regal texture. The paper wraps around the cardboard in layered sections, and additional pieces of ballot box paper were cut and arranged to add visual variety and detail across the crown. Two wires were incorporated into the design to give the piece a touch of class, rising from the top and adding a sense of height and elegance. They catch the light in a way that makes the whole sculpture feel a little more alive.

To ensure the crown could actually be worn without falling apart, I reinforced the interior with strips of Shin Ramen cardboard. The ramen box cardboard is surprisingly sturdy, and wrapping it around the inside of the crown gave the structure the strength it needed to sit comfortably on my head. There is something playful about the idea that a piece of food packaging is quietly holding the whole thing together.

Looking back honestly, I think I could have pushed this piece further if I had spent more time hunting for better recyclable materials. The materials I found worked and they gave the crown its own scrappy charm, but with a wider search I could have discovered textures and surfaces that elevated the whole piece even more. That said, working within those constraints taught me something valuable about resourcefulness and making the most of what is in front of you.

I am still missing a photograph of myself wearing the crown, which I plan to take soon against a clean background as required. But even without that image yet, the crown stands on its own as something I am genuinely proud of. It falls under the category of extension, as it sits on top of the body and amplifies presence and identity. Wearing it changes how I carry myself and how others perceive me.

More than anything, this crown is a relic from my childhood. It is a physical reminder of the happiness that Technoblade brought into my life and the lives of so many others. Making it wearable again was not just an assignment. It was a chance to revisit something meaningful and carry it forward. The process of building it filled me with the same excitement I felt as a kid watching those videos, and that is exactly the kind of energy I wanted this sculpture to hold.
