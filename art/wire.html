Hanging by the Edge

A suspended human figure made from coiled copper wire. I used an open structure and a tangled head to explore urgency, vulnerability and the cost of leaving too little time to experiment.

Artist statement

My work begins with self-awareness and the strange electricity of doing things at the last possible moment. Through coiling and bending copper wire, I developed a sculptural figure that is open and vulnerable. The human form stands wound tight, its tangled head a portrait of thoughts that circle without settling. This piece explores urgency and the tension between excitement and loss, interacting with space by drawing lines into air rather than building solid mass. I became interested in the honest question this sculpture forced me to confront, what do I miss by never arriving early enough to truly explore? The relationship between structure and vulnerability is what holds this figure upright, both literally and as an idea.

Reflection

There is something quietly honest about calling a piece "Hanging by the Edge." I have always lived in that precarious last moment before things are due, chasing the strange electricity that urgency seems to generate in me. But this sculpture forced me to sit with a question I usually avoid: what do I lose by never arriving early enough to truly explore?

I wanted the work to represent something real about myself, so I shaped a human figure from copper wire. Copper felt right because of how willingly it bends, how it almost meets you halfway when you're trying to coax something recognizable out of raw material. The coiling was both instinctive and deliberate. Coils are among my favorite structures to observe, the way they fold energy into themselves, and wrapping the body in them felt like an accurate portrait of that tense, wound-up state I inhabit when I am teetering at the edge of a deadline. The head, a dense tangle of loops, became something like a self portrait of my thoughts, restless and circular and never quite settling. Working in wire taught me that structure and vulnerability are not opposites. Sometimes the most honest form is also the most open one.
