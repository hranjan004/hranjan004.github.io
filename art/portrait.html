Self Portrait

A book, a pen and a mirror stand in for absorbing information, turning it into thought and examining the self. I used their arrangement to explore the relationship between what we take in and what we put back into the world.

Artist statement

My work begins with personal inventory and the courage of looking inward without flinching. Through careful arrangement and photography, I developed a still life that is intimate and deliberately symbolic. A book, a pen, and a mirror sit together, each one standing in for something I wanted to examine about myself. This piece explores identity and projection, interacting with space by asking the viewer to read objects as quiet confessions. I became interested in how the mirror complicates everything, representing a tendency toward self-obsession that I want to move past. The relationship between what we absorb from the world and what we project back onto it is what this piece keeps asking me to think about.

Reflection

The three objects I chose are a book, a pen, and a mirror. The book is one I keep on my desk, a constant companion through coursework and curiosity alike. The pen is ordinary, the kind I reach for without thinking when I need to write something down. The mirror is small and personal, something I have looked into more times than I can count.

I chose these objects as a self-portrait because a literal image of myself would only show what is visible on the surface. These three objects reveal something truer about how I move through the world and what I struggle with internally. A photograph of my face cannot capture the tension between who I am and who I am trying to become.

The book represents what matters most to me. I love to listen to others and to absorb information at any cost, to keep taking things in all the time and to learn something new every day. It speaks to a deep cultural value I hold around knowledge and growth, the idea that we are shaped by what we allow into our minds. The pen represents what I aspire to do with all of this input. I want to streamline the information I take in through different sources into crystal clear narrowed down writable thoughts and actions, so that they do not clutter my mind unnecessarily and are not just randomly available in my head. The pen is discipline, the act of making sense out of noise. The mirror represents my weakness. It stands for self-obsession and self-centeredness, the tendency that pulls me back and makes me take lacklustre decisions where I isolate myself unnecessarily. I want to get out of the self-made images of myself that I create and that I think I continuously project around people.

In the arrangement, both the pen and the mirror stand on top of the book, and the pen is positioned looking into the mirror. Each of these objects is interconnected in a deep inexplicable way, continuously competing for my attention. The book serves as the foundation, the base upon which everything else rests, because without the constant intake of information and listening to others, neither reflection nor refinement would have any material to work with. The pen looks into the mirror judging itself before it refines what it has come up with. This captures the tension I live with daily. Before I can distill my thoughts into something clear and actionable, I find myself caught in self-scrutiny, questioning whether what I am about to put down is good enough or true enough. The mirror tempts the pen into hesitation. And yet they all need each other. The book feeds the pen, the mirror forces the pen to confront itself, and the pen ultimately must move past that reflection to create something real. What I hope for is that the pen learns to glance at the mirror without getting trapped in it, to use that moment of self-awareness as a tool for clarity rather than a reason to stop writing altogether.
