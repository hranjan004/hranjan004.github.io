Sources of Law and the Limits of Autonomy

A series examining the gap between legal recognition and lived autonomy in China and the United States.

An overview of the related Indigenous-rights and sources-of-law essays recorded in The Complete Record. Full drafts are not reproduced here.

Across several drafts, I examined how legal systems describe autonomy and how institutions limit it in practice. The American discussion focused on tribal sovereignty and federal power; the Chinese discussion examined formal autonomy within a centralized party-state.

The series asks how sources of law can legitimize subordination even when their language promises recognition. I approached the question through constitutional texts, legal doctrines and institutional comparisons, keeping the gap between formal rights and their operation at the center.
